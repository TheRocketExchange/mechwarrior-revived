# Design notes

Revived is a modernized, fan-made take on MechWarrior: Dark Age, using existing MW:DA clix figures and their combat dials. BattleMechs, vehicles, and infantry fight for ground. The aim is a game of about two hours in which infantry matter for the ground they hold and the attacks they make.

## Roles on the table

| Unit | Job | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conventional infantry | Hold cover and objectives; assault exposed armor | Vulnerable in the open; little damage against larger targets |
| Battle armor | Fight vehicles and ’Mechs up close | Must reach them; gains less from fieldworks |
| Vehicles | Move and fire; carry infantry | Cannot fight in close combat |
| ’Mechs | Bring firepower where it is needed | Heat forces pauses; damage weakens the dial |

## What stays

Keep the figures, bases, ranges, arcs, combat values, and changing dials. Keep each ’Mech’s heat track and vent rating. Infantry in a squad still use their own values.

Attacks keep the original three dice and white-dice critical results. See the [source notes](sources.md).

## What changes

| Rule | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Alternate one ’Mech, vehicle, or squad; everyone acts once per round | Keep both players involved |
| Fixed squads of 2–3 infantry bases, with one shared order | Reduce waiting and make weapon positions a squad decision |
| Advance shoots after a half-speed move, with no general attack penalty | Keep mobile fire while making prepared positions matter |
| Fire allows a pivot before close combat; ranged Fire keeps its facing | Let engaged units face their opponent without choosing Assault |
| Death from Above trades a full-speed jump and +2 damage for +2 target defense, landing damage, and heat | Bring back a risky jump attack with one roll and no displacement |
| Combat orders commit then push vehicles and squads; Move or Hold recovers | Restore pauses and decisions about spending the damage dial |
| Each base attacks separately, without ally accuracy bonuses | Let infantry earn their place through their own actions |
| Infantry gain stronger ranged cover; fieldworks also protect against close attacks | Make infantry positions harder to clear while leaving open ground dangerous |
| Infantry close assaults against exposed armor | Make terrain and timing matter |
| Heat comes from actions; Hold and Vent cool | Fit heat to alternating activations |
| Printed heat modifiers and one stress roll for colored warnings | Keep different heat tracks with fewer lookups |
| Six equipment colors with new meanings | Use one reference across collections |
| Ballistic recoil, Energy heat, and stationary Rocket fire | Give each weapon a strength and a cost |
| Up to two free carriers, each holding one squad | Make transport easy to test with existing figures |
| Scenario-specific victory | Keep attention on the mission |
| Four pilot roles, one included in each ’Mech’s cost | Add character without advancement |
| One points formula for every unit | Price each complete click, giving early strength more weight while charging for how well the unit holds up |
| Four game sizes with upper limits by unit type and three choices per chassis or infantry family | Support smaller and larger collections, including forces without ’Mechs, while limiting repetition |

There are no campaigns, pilot levels, custom units, or expansion rules in this version.

## What needs testing

Every unit still activates each round, but vehicles and infantry now need recovery. Check whether pushing creates useful urgent attacks and whether Move lets recovery serve the mission. Cheaper support units may increase army size. Cheap squads may still let a player delay important activations. Check cover, transport survival, and two- versus three-base squads.

The [combat examples](combat-rhythm.md) explain the expected effects and the four-case comparison. Expanded original heat effects, equipment meanings, and more pilot abilities belong in a later pass.

The shared points formula is a starting estimate. The sample armies use several chassis and follow the Standard force limits. Test other chassis and heat tracks too, then adjust the shared weights if results show a consistent problem. Games at 600, 1,200, 1,800, and 2,400 points have their own unit, carrier, and pilot limits. The rule of three stays fixed. Larger tables gain width while retaining the 48-inch depth, so mission travel distances stay the same. Test each size’s force variety, activation counts, flank use, and play time.

Use the [playtest plan](../playtest/protocol.md), repeat games with sides reversed, and record time and rules lookups. Balance at each size and Standard’s two-hour target remain untested.

## Introducing the game

The opening explains the battle, player decisions, and turn sequence before asking readers to build an army. The full force chart follows the play rules; sample armies let a new player start sooner.

Presentation references:

- [Mantic’s Firefight introduction](https://www.manticgames.com/what-is-firefight/) establishes the setting and alternating actions before its getting-started material.
- [The NetEA Tournament Pack](https://tp.net-armageddon.org/tournament-pack/) teaches core play and training scenarios before its army-list sections.
- [Warhammer 40,000’s March 2023 army-building guide](https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/z6UkH6T3/how-army-building-works-in-the-new-edition-of-warhammer-40000/) gives a clear sequence from battle size to unit selection and states repetition limits. Revived applies its own limits to chassis and infantry squads.

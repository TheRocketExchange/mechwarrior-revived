# MechWarrior Revived core rules

Version 0.2 · Playtest rules

Use your existing MechWarrior clix figures and the numbers on their dials. These rules cover a two-player game with 4–8 ’Mechs per side, plus vehicles and infantry.

If rules disagree, follow the scenario for deployment and victory, then any specific pilot or equipment rule, then the core rules.

## 1 Assemble forces

Start with a 1,200-point limit. Add the **Revived points** from the [unit catalog](../site/units.html), stay under the limit, and aim for opposing totals within 2%. Pay for every base in a squad. All costs use the same [points formula](points.md); the original printed cost is only a reference. Use one formula version for both armies.

You may mix factions and take duplicates. A figure marked Unique is limited to one of that collector’s number per side.

Use ordinary or quad ’Mechs, ground vehicles, conventional infantry, and battle armor. Treat quads as ’Mechs. Identify battle armor by the unit’s name or listing, not its equipment colors; other infantry count as conventional.

Leave out aircraft, artillery-only and aquatic-only units, colossal ’Mechs, DropShips, and units that need expansion rules. Both players may agree to use an artillery-marked unit as a ground vehicle if it has ordinary printed ranges. It gets no artillery attack.

**Infantry squads.** Group each infantry type into squads of two or three bases. Keep conventional infantry and battle armor separate. A one-base squad is allowed only if you have just one base of that type. Record each squad’s members; they cannot split, merge, or switch squads during play. Casualties may leave a lone survivor.

**Carriers.** Choose up to two ground vehicles as carriers, at no extra cost. Each holds one squad of up to three bases, including battle armor. Ignore printed transport ratings. Record the carriers and any starting passengers.

**Pilots.** Give each ’Mech one [Revived pilot](../pilots/pilot-cards.md), with at most two of each role per force. Its cost already includes the pilot allowance. A pilot affects only its own ’Mech and is lost with it. Use the new cards, without original pilot or gear cards.

**Weapons.** Label each ranged damage value Energy, Ballistic, or Rocket before deployment. Use the [weapon reference](weapon-reference.md) to identify them or mark a Rocket test substitute. A substitute uses its alternate cost on the datasheet. Keep labels and costs for the whole game. There is no ammunition tracking.

## 2 Prepare the table

You need a 48 × 48 inch table, rulers, terrain, objective markers, pilot cards, and a marker for each group that has acted. Use three six-sided dice: two white and one other color. Keep the round and score on paper. Each [scenario](../scenarios/01-seize-and-hold.md) gives its terrain layout and deployment zones.

Set combat dials to their starting marks and heat dials to their green starting positions. If a dial is jammed, use a written copy of its track. Agree on terrain boundaries and unclear colors before deploying.

Unless the scenario says otherwise:

1. Roll one die each and reroll ties. The higher roller chooses north or south.
2. The other player places a group first. Alternate until both armies are deployed. A carrier and its starting passengers are one placement.
3. Keep each base entirely inside its deployment zone, in terrain it can enter. Keep squads together and choose each unit’s facing.

Deployment order does not decide who acts first.

## 3 Rounds and activation

An **activation group** is one ’Mech, one vehicle, or one infantry squad. Each surviving group acts once per round.

1. Remove activation markers. Roll one die each and reroll ties. The winner chooses who goes first.
2. Take turns activating one group that has not acted. Mark it when finished.
3. Once every group has acted, score the scenario, check for victory, and begin the next round.

For a squad, finish one base’s action before starting the next. Each may choose its own action and target. Read the target’s new dial after every attack. Destroyed units cannot act.

**Passing:** You may pass only when you have fewer groups left to activate than your opponent. If you have none, you must pass. Count passengers and shutdown ’Mechs: they can Hold aboard or Vent. Passing does not use a group’s activation.

There is no pushing damage for acting in consecutive rounds, and no cooling at round end. If neither side has units left on the board or aboard carriers, score that round and use the scenario’s final result.

### Keeping squads together

At deployment and at the end of an activation, a squad must form one connected chain. Neighboring base centers can be no more than 4 inches apart. Bases may move out of that chain while the squad is acting, but must finish together.

If casualties or a destroyed carrier leave a squad split, it must **regroup** next activation. Each base may move up to its speed, or Disengage if touching an enemy. It cannot attack, repair, board, or score an exit. Rejoin the chain if possible; otherwise close the gaps as much as possible. No one takes damage for being out of formation. A lone survivor always counts as together.

## 4 Actions

Each base takes one action per activation. **Speed** means its current printed speed plus any heat modifier, never less than zero. Round half speed up. Add movement bonuses from equipment after halving or doubling speed. You may move less than the allowance.

| Action | What you do | ’Mech heat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fire | Stay still; make one ranged or close attack | +1 |
| Advance | Move up to your speed, then make one ranged attack | +2 |
| Rush | Move up to twice your speed; no attack | +1 |
| Assault | Infantry or ’Mech: move up to half speed, then make one close attack | +1 |
| Disengage | Leave enemy contact; move up to half speed; no attack | +1 |
| Hold | Stay still; no attack | Cool 1 |
| Vent | ’Mech: stay still; cool by your printed vent rating, at least 1 | Cooling only |
| Repair | Use gray attack equipment; stay still and repair one friendly unit | +1 |
| Embark or Disembark | Board or leave a carrier with the whole squad; see Transport | None |

Advance has no attack penalty of its own. Moving or turning before a Ballistic shot causes −1 attack for recoil; Gunner can remove it during a half-speed Advance. Rockets can shoot only with Fire.

A ’Mech adds **1 extra heat** if it rolls an Energy attack. Add this to the action heat, even on a miss. Pay the action’s heat even if you skip a shot or cannot make it; add no Energy heat without a roll. Destroyed ’Mechs take no further heat.

You may check whether an action is possible before choosing it. If you declare an action you cannot finish, carry out the legal parts, lose any unavailable attack, and pay its heat.

## 5 Movement and facing

Measure distances between base centers unless a rule says the bases must touch. Measure movement along the route taken. Your whole base must fit along that route: it cannot overlap another base, cross blocking terrain, or squeeze through a gap narrower than itself. This includes friendly bases.

You may turn during any action that allows movement, even if you move no distance. You cannot turn during Fire, Hold, Vent, or Repair, or in response to an enemy action.

Infantry can attack in any direction and have no rear arc. Vehicles and ’Mechs use their printed front and rear arcs. If a rear arc is missing, agree on a rear 90-degree arc before play. A line exactly on its boundary gets no rear bonus.

### Enemy contact

Stop when your base touches an enemy. You are now **engaged**.

While engaged, you may Fire a close attack, Assault in place, Hold, Vent, or Disengage. An Assault may turn to face the enemy but cannot move away. Vehicles cannot make close attacks. You cannot shoot, repair, Advance, Rush, board, or unload normally while engaged.

To **Disengage**, take 1 unavoidable damage before moving if any touching enemy is not shutdown. Pay once, however many enemies touch you. If destroyed, remove the unit. Otherwise move up to half speed, without touching a new enemy. A pilot or equipment may remove the damage cost. There is no damage cost if all touching enemies are shutdown.

A unit with zero speed cannot change position. It may turn with Assault; if it has no legal attack, Hold or Vent instead.

Stay on the board unless a scenario allows an exit. If the sculpts prevent bases from touching, agree where the bases would meet; use the bases, not overhanging models, for measurements.

## 6 Terrain and sight

Mark each terrain area clearly. A unit is in terrain when its base center is inside or on the edge. Blocking terrain is different: no part of a base may overlap it.

Each inch moved through difficult terrain costs 2 inches of movement; other ground costs 1. Do not round these distances. Ignore height, roofs, building damage, and deep water.

| Terrain | Movement | Cover and sight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open ground or road | Normal | No cover |
| Woods or rubble | Difficult for everyone | Ranged defense: +2 for conventional infantry, +1 for others |
| Fieldworks | Infantry only; normal movement | Conventional infantry: +3 ranged defense and +1 close defense. Battle armor: +2 ranged defense |
| Blocking building or rock | Impassable | Blocks direct line of sight |
| Shallow water | Difficult, except for hover vehicles | No cover or extra cooling |

Fieldworks are trenches or narrow ruins. Vehicles and ’Mechs may touch their boundary, but no part of their base may enter or cross the interior. Leave room to go around.

**Line of sight:** Draw a line between attacker and target centers. It must pass through the attacker’s front arc, unless the attacker is infantry. A line that crosses or just touches blocking terrain is blocked. Woods and rubble do not block sight.

Intervening ’Mechs block direct sight. Vehicles also block it when neither attacker nor target is a ’Mech. Infantry never block sight. Indirect Rocket fire ignores these blockers.

Never shoot at a friendly unit or an enemy touching a friendly unit, even with indirect fire.

**Cover:** Use the target’s terrain, not terrain crossed by the shot. Take the single best cover bonus, up to +3 ranged defense. Equipment defense is separate. Cover does not protect against close attacks, except conventional infantry’s +1 defense in fieldworks.

## 7 Combat

Each base makes its own attacks. Every attack has one enemy target and one roll; ignore extra target symbols. Units never improve another unit’s accuracy. There are no combined attacks, spotting bonuses, reaction shots, captures, rams, charge damage, or death-from-above attacks.

For a ranged attack, choose one printed damage value and its matching range and weapon type. A target exactly at minimum or maximum range is in range. Melee weapons cannot shoot.

For a close attack, infantry use their damage value. ’Mechs use primary damage or a value marked melee. The front arc must touch the target; infantry may attack in any direction. Vehicles cannot make close attacks. Ranged weapon benefits and costs do not apply to close attacks.

You need printed attack above zero and damage above zero after heat to attack. A unit with zero attack or damage may still move, carry troops, and contest objectives.

Read values and equipment when declaring an attack. Use them for that whole attack, then read the dials again for the next one.

### Ranged weapon types

| Type | Benefit | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ballistic | +1 damage against conventional infantry or battle armor with no terrain cover | −1 attack if you moved or turned before firing |
| Energy | Piercing: ignore 1 point of armor | Firing ’Mechs gain 1 extra heat. Damage against conventional infantry is limited to 2 before armor |
| Rocket | Can fire indirectly through terrain and intervening bases | Fire action only. Indirect shots take −2 attack and −1 damage |

For the Ballistic bonus, check the target’s cover before any equipment reduces or ignores it. Ballistic and Rocket shots add no extra weapon heat. Energy’s damage limit applies to all firers, including those that do not track heat.

Declare a Rocket shot direct or indirect before rolling. Direct shots follow normal sight rules. Indirect shots ignore blockers, but still need the correct arc and range. The attacker must be unengaged, and the target cannot touch a friendly unit. Keep the target’s cover; indirect fire gets no rear bonus. It needs no spotter and hits only one target.

See the [weapon reference](weapon-reference.md) for labels and examples.

### Attack sequence

1. Choose the action, target, weapon, and any pilot or equipment effects. Check range, facing, sight, and contact.
2. Roll all three dice. Add their total to your attack value and modifiers. Equal or beat the target’s defense to hit.
3. **Both white dice show 6:** hit automatically and add 1 damage. **Both show 1:** miss automatically and take 1 unavoidable damage. The third die does not change either result.
4. On a hit, work out damage below and click the target’s combat dial. Stop at three bullet holes: the unit is destroyed. Remove it; never click past destruction.
5. Apply any heat dealt to the target by equipment, including its stress check. Then apply the attacker’s heat. A surviving attacker still pays heat after a critical miss.

| Situation | Modifier |
| --- | --- |
| Advance | No attack penalty |
| Ballistic shot after moving or turning | −1 attack for recoil; Gunner may remove it |
| Indirect Rocket shot | −2 attack |
| Shot enters a vehicle or ’Mech’s rear arc, or close attack touches its rear | +2 attack; not for indirect fire |
| Shutdown target | −2 defense |
| Target in cover | Use the terrain table |
| Your pilot or equipment | Use its card or reference |

Rear and shutdown modifiers add together. There are no extra modifiers for range bands, a moving target, nearby allies, or squad size.

### Damage and infantry assaults

Work out damage in this order:

1. Printed damage plus its heat modifier, at least zero.
2. Weapon bonus or penalty, then equipment and pilot bonuses (**+1 combined maximum**). Add battle armor’s close bonus and any critical-hit bonus separately. Damage cannot fall below zero.
3. Apply the damage limits below, including any critical bonus.
4. Subtract armor, after Piercing. Armor reduces damage by at most 1. Piercing ignores that 1; extra sources of Piercing add nothing. Damage may be zero.

| Attack | Damage limit or bonus |
| --- | --- |
| Conventional infantry against a vehicle or ’Mech | At most 1 damage before armor |
| Exposed close assault by conventional infantry | At most 2 before armor, with Piercing |
| Battle armor close attack against a vehicle or ’Mech | +1 damage and Piercing |
| Energy shot against conventional infantry | At most 2 before armor |

An infantry close assault is **exposed** if the attacker touches the target’s rear, the target is shutdown, or the attacker stands in woods, rubble, or fieldworks when attacking. Other attacks have no damage limit based on unit type. Energy does not heat the target automatically.

**Example:** Conventional infantry with damage 3 hit an armored ’Mech’s open front. The limit of 1, then Armor 1, leaves no damage. Attacking from the rear raises the limit to 2 and adds Piercing: 2 damage. Battle armor with damage 2 deals 3 in close combat against that ’Mech, even from the front.

## 8 Heat

’Mechs use their printed heat tracks. Apply the printed speed and damage modifiers to the matching values, never below zero. Use the rules here instead of the original named heat-color effects. Infantry and vehicles ignore heat.

After an action, add its heat and any Energy or Jump heat together. Turn the heat dial one click at a time. Stop at the first position showing three shutdown symbols and discard excess heat. Never wrap around to cold.

**Stress:** After each complete heat gain, check the dial. If the ’Mech is not shutdown and any heat background is colored other than green or plain white, roll one die:

- **1:** Take 1 unavoidable combat-dial damage.
- **2–6:** No effect.

Roll only once, even with several colored warnings. A number without a colored background does not trigger stress. Stress damage adds no heat. Cooling never causes a stress roll.

**Shutdown:** A ’Mech showing three shutdown symbols can only Vent. It cannot move, turn, attack, control or contest objectives, or use pilots other than Engineer. Passive defense equipment still works. Enemies get the shutdown defense modifier and exposed-assault benefit.

**Cooling:** Hold cools 1. Vent cools the printed vent rating, at least 1; Engineer adds 1. Turn toward the green starting position and stop there. When the shutdown symbols disappear, the ’Mech restarts, but its activation is already spent. There is no extra cooling at round end.

## 9 Transport

Each carrier holds one whole infantry squad. No partial squads, mixed squads, vehicles, or ’Mechs may ride. Keep passengers beside the roster with their current dials.

Passengers may Hold or Disembark on their squad’s activation. They cannot attack, repair, be targeted, use equipment, or control objectives while aboard. The carrier moves and fires normally.

### Embark

Activate the squad. Each base may move up to its speed without attacking. To board:

- Every surviving member must finish within 3 inches of the same empty friendly carrier’s center.
- The squad must form its usual 4-inch chain.
- Neither the carrier nor the squad may touch an enemy.

Remove the squad and mark it activated. If any condition fails, the bases stay where they finished moving and their activation is spent. The carrier may take its own activation before or after boarding.

### Disembark

Activate the passenger squad. Its carrier must not touch an enemy. Place the whole squad within 3 inches of the carrier’s center, in legal terrain, in its 4-inch chain, and outside enemy contact.

This uses the squad’s activation: no further move, attack, or boarding. If the whole squad cannot fit, it stays aboard and Holds. The carrier’s activation is unaffected.

### Carrier destroyed

Finish the attack, then give each passenger 1 unavoidable damage. Remove casualties and the carrier. Place survivors within 3 inches of the carrier’s former center, in legal terrain and outside enemy contact; you may use the space the carrier occupied.

Save as many bases as can fit. Remove any that cannot be placed. Mark the surviving squad activated, even if it had not acted. If it is split, it must regroup next round. Leave no wreck.

A squad cannot board and unload in the same round or gain another activation when its carrier is destroyed. In Breakthrough, passengers exit with their carrier and score their own Revived points once.

## 10 Objectives and victory

You control an objective when at least one of your base centers is within 3 inches and no enemy base center is. If both sides are present, it is **contested** and neither controls it.

Shutdown ’Mechs, passengers, and units that have left the board do not count. Engaged units and units with zero attack or damage still count. Squad members may contest different sites if they keep their 4-inch chain. Moving away gives up control.

The scenario sets when to score, how the game ends, and how to handle ties. Destroying units earns no points unless the scenario says so. Read the victory conditions before deployment.

If you run out of time, finish the round only if both players can. Otherwise record an unfinished game and the last fully scored round. Record concessions as concessions, not completed games.

# Playtest plan

Version 0.3

Keep the rules, game size, points formula, rosters, squads, and pilots the same for each game and its return match. Save proposed changes until both games are finished. No tabletop results have been recorded yet.

For a learning session, play [First Contact](../scenarios/00-first-contact.md) with its [starter patrols](starter-forces.md). Play a four-round game and return match, using the map and teaching diagrams. Record starter games separately from the full-mission matrix below.

The [Dead Air playtest pack](dead-air.md) collects that matchup with named patrols, a map, assigned cards and a one-page results sheet. Keep the same PDF for both games; its snapshot identifies the rules and model data used. The story adds no special rules.

## Try these first

Use real bases and current dials. Reset damage and heat between exercises.

| Try | Watch for |
| --- | --- |
| The same conventional squad in the open, woods, fieldworks, and close combat from the rear | Hits, damage, survival, and whether the position is reachable |
| Battle armor making the same close attacks | Its advantage against armor and shutdown targets; Piercing and critical hits |
| Both infantry types defending woods, rubble, and fieldworks against shooting and close attacks | Infantry ranged cover +3/+4; battle armor +2/+3. Fieldworks close defense +2/+1. Check equipment stacking, passive protection during Salvage, and unchanged exposure in the open |
| Fire while engaged with an enemy behind you, including at zero speed | Pivot without moving the center or breaking contact; no Assault needed; ranged Fire still cannot pivot |
| Ordinary jumping Assault versus DFA against the same vehicle or ’Mech | Half-speed versus full-speed reach; DFA +2 defense/+2 damage; landing damage on a hit, miss, or critical miss; 2 total heat |
| DFA on the last live click; a miss beside a surviving target; blocked landing; Jump lost on the dial | Finish target effects before landing damage; no heat after destruction; no displacement or reactive pivot; legal start and landing required |
| Two rounds with six infantry in two-base squads, then three-base squads | Passes, waiting time, and how long a squad takes |
| Board then move; move then unload; destroy a loaded carrier before and after passengers act; block the unloading area | Placement, losses, and whether anyone accidentally gets an extra action |
| Repeated Advance with two different heat tracks, then Vent from shutdown | Heat modifiers, stress rolls, and restart timing |
| Damage through an equipment change, then attack again; Repair to a black mark | Use the new equipment on the next attack; never repair past the mark |
| Each pilot, including Brawler with damage equipment and Engineer during shutdown | Whether the benefit works and is counted only once |
| Each weapon against open infantry, covered infantry, armor, and targets behind buildings | Recoil, heat, damage limits, and direct versus indirect fire |
| Fire versus Advance; Gunner just within and just beyond half speed, counting terrain | No general Advance penalty; Ballistic −1 recoil unless Gunner applies; Energy Advance costs 3 heat; Rockets cannot Advance-fire |

Record how Rocket weapons were identified or marked as substitutes.

## Compare v0.2 and v0.3

Before the full matrix, use one matchup and map for the four cases in the [combat-rhythm examples](../docs/combat-rhythm.md): v0.2, readiness only, half-speed Advance only, and full v0.3. Start with the current legal sample lists and play a return game for each case. Keep the exact figures, pilots, squad membership, terrain, and role pairings; do not add models to spend new cheaper prices yet. Calculate these figures’ costs with the archived v0.2 formula and use those same totals and Breakthrough thresholds across the four cases. Keep the current force limits in every case so changes in army organization do not affect this comparison.

Cases that introduce readiness also introduce Move and its recovery, passenger, and exit rules. The movement-only case adds Move without readiness. In full v0.3, squads also use a common order. Log situations where that changes choices; if needed, repeat full v0.3 with separate squad orders to measure coordination on its own.

Then rebuild to the v0.3 limit using the new costs and repeat the v0.2/v0.3 comparison, with each edition’s own roster totals and escape threshold. This separates the rule changes from the extra models a new price may buy. Keep a single formula version within each game and return pair.

Use these additional short exercises before the first v0.3 game:

| Try | Expected result |
| --- | --- |
| Fire, Fire, Move; then Fire, Hold, Fire | First sequence pushes once and recovers; second preserves the dial |
| Rush or Repair while Committed | State stays Committed; the next combat order pushes |
| A failed or declined attack while pushing | State becomes Spent; every surviving base still pays |
| Critical miss while pushing, with two clicks remaining then one | Two clicks: pay miss then push and die. One click: die on the miss, no extra cost transferred |
| Committed mixed-weapon squad chooses Advance with one blocked shot and one Rocket | Separate legal moves/shots; all survivors pay pushing damage |
| Spent squad with one engaged member | Engaged base Holds or Disengages; others Move or Hold; all keep the chain |
| Split Spent squad near an exit or carrier | Regroup clears readiness but cannot exit or board; repeat regroup if still split |
| Hold aboard, failed unload, forced evacuation | Only Hold clears passenger readiness; carrier state stays separate |
| Speed 7 with Jump Jets; walk or jump through woods | Advance allowance 4; walking 2 inches or jumping 4 both cost 4; Jump adds 1 heat |
| Spent squad exits with Move | Allowed only when unengaged and all survivors can leave together; no regroup exit |

Log actual attack rolls, recovery activations, pushing damage, losses caused by pushing, objective results, and minutes. Write each vehicle or squad’s action sequence so repeated Fire/Move and Fire/Fire/Hold patterns are visible. Include useful recovery moves and turns spent with no legal shot. These exercises and model outputs are not completed tabletop games.

## Equipment exercises

Check Salvage on a damaged attacker; a green-Attack repair crew; Command linking two unused activations without granting a second turn; Bypass capture and recapture; armor grades against Energy and Armor Piercing; and Pulse/Rapid Strike losing their second attack when the target dies. Try ECM against Streak, Flamers against infantry and a shutdown Mech, and infantry squads hit by TSEMP. Log how often support abilities and extra targets are actually available.

## Full games

Use the [sample forces](sample-forces.md): H (’Mech-heavy), B (balanced), and I (infantry-heavy). Play every matchup below twice, following the scenario’s instructions for exchanging sides or roles. That makes **9 pairs, 18 games**.

This matrix uses **Standard, 1,200 points**. To test another size, build two legal lists for it and play a return pair. Keep the prescribed battlefield for that size, including the added flanks at 1,800 and 2,400 points. Record the size, table dimensions, activation counts, time per round, and whether deployment or objectives feel crowded. Compare results within each size before combining them; the larger sizes have no recorded play-time estimates yet.

| --- | --- | --- |
| SH-HB, SH-BI, SH-HI | Seize and Hold | H/B, B/I, H/I |
| BT-HB, BT-BI, BT-HI | Breakthrough | H/B, B/I, H/I |

Add A to the first game’s ID and B to the return match. Roll first-game sides normally. Keep terrain and rosters fixed. If practical, exchange players’ armies too, to reduce the effect of familiarity.

Afterward, repeat at least one pair with other chassis and heat tracks from your collection, staying within the force limits. Then change one thing at a time. Try squad size first if waiting remains a problem. To test without pilots, remove them from both armies and label that pair as a separate experiment.

## What to record

Use [one log per game](playtest-log.md). Leave missing measurements as “unknown.” Record setup and teaching separately from play time; include rules lookups in play time.

| Question | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Do infantry matter? | Infantry should affect scoring, denial, screening, or assaults in at least one game of each pair where used. Review pairs where they do nothing |
| Does position matter? | Cover should help survival; exposed assaults should matter; frontal attacks on armor should remain weak |
| Do vehicles and ’Mechs have jobs? | Record useful transport, fire support, movement, and objective play in each pair |
| Do squads reduce waiting? | Count passes and activations after the opponent has finished. Review repeated rounds with 3 or more unanswered activations, or missions decided by holding infantry back solely to delay |
| Is the game a reasonable length? | For Standard games, aim for median play time of 120 minutes or less and review games over 150 minutes. Record other sizes separately. Review waits longer than 5 minutes at any size |
| Is the quick reference enough? | Aim for no more than 6 core-rule lookups after round 2 in a typical game. Add repeatedly missing details to the reference |
| Does one side have too much advantage? | Compare return matches. Review a role or edge that wins all three matchup pairs without a clear army or tactical reason |
| Are the rules clear? | Record any transport, heat, equipment, or pilot dispute needed to finish play |
| Are all weapons worth using? | Record targets, movement, and weapon choices. Check whether Energy feels compulsory or Rockets are too safe behind buildings |
| Do the force limits help? | Record vehicle and squad counts, repeated families, and legal lists players wanted to field but could not. Check whether the caps produce varied armies without crowding out a unit type |
| Does the dial pricing fit actual play? | Record clicks used for attacks, how soon strong starting values are lost, and any late recovery in performance. Compare quick decline with steady dials at similar costs; check whether the 1.5-to-0.5 weighting overvalues opening strength |

Measure each player’s longest stretch without a game decision, excluding agreed breaks.

These are prompts for review, not proof of balance. Identify whether a problem comes from wording, the scenario, a pilot, or a unit before changing points.

## Between games

For a rules gap, write down what happened and both players’ temporary ruling. Use that ruling in both games and mark the pair as a rules-clarification test.

Between pairs, update the version and any affected references or cards. Record formula changes and their reasons in [adjustments.csv](adjustments.csv), then rebuild all costs together.

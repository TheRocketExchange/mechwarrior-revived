Playtest plan

v0.3 · Playtest

Version 0.3

Keep the rules, points formula, rosters, squads, and pilots the same for each game and its return match. Save proposed changes until both games are finished. No tabletop results have been recorded yet.

Try these first

Use real bases and current dials. Reset damage and heat between exercises.

Try Watch for
The same conventional squad in the open, woods, fieldworks, and close combat from the rear Hits, damage, survival, and whether the position is reachable
Battle armor making the same close attacks Its advantage against armor and shutdown targets; Piercing and critical hits
Two rounds with six infantry in two-base squads, then three-base squads Passes, waiting time, and how long a squad takes
Board then move; move then unload; destroy a loaded carrier before and after passengers act; block the unloading area Placement, losses, and whether anyone accidentally gets an extra action
Repeated Advance with two different heat tracks, then Vent from shutdown Heat modifiers, stress rolls, and restart timing
Damage through an equipment change, then attack again; Repair to a black mark Use the new equipment on the next attack; never repair past the mark
Each pilot, including Brawler with damage equipment and Engineer during shutdown Whether the benefit works and is counted only once
Each weapon against open infantry, covered infantry, armor, and targets behind buildings Recoil, heat, damage limits, and direct versus indirect fire
Fire versus Advance; Gunner just within and just beyond half speed, counting terrain No general Advance penalty; Ballistic −1 recoil unless Gunner applies; Energy Advance costs 3 heat; Rockets cannot Advance-fire

Record how Rocket weapons were identified or marked as substitutes.

Compare v0.2 and v0.3

Before the full matrix, use one matchup and map for the four cases in the combat-rhythm examples: v0.2, readiness only, half-speed Advance only, and full v0.3. Play a return game for each case. Keep the exact figures, pilots, squad membership, terrain, and role pairings; do not add models to spend new cheaper prices yet. Use the same recorded v0.2 roster totals and Breakthrough threshold across these fixed-model tests.

Cases that introduce readiness also introduce Move and its recovery, passenger, and exit rules. The movement-only case adds Move without readiness. In full v0.3, squads also use a common order. Log situations where that changes choices; if needed, repeat full v0.3 with separate squad orders to measure coordination on its own.

Then rebuild to the v0.3 limit using the new costs and repeat the v0.2/v0.3 comparison, with each edition’s own roster totals and escape threshold. This separates the rule changes from the extra models a new price may buy. Keep a single formula version within each game and return pair.

Log actual attack rolls, recovery activations, pushing damage, losses caused by pushing, objective results, and minutes. These exercises and model outputs are not completed tabletop games.

Full games

Use the sample forces: H (’Mech-heavy), B (balanced), and I (infantry-heavy). Play every matchup below twice, following the scenario’s instructions for exchanging sides or roles. That makes 9 pairs, 18 games.

Game IDs Scenario Matchups
SH-HB, SH-BI, SH-HI Seize and Hold H/B, B/I, H/I
BT-HB, BT-BI, BT-HI Breakthrough H/B, B/I, H/I
HS-HB, HS-BI, HS-HI Hold the Station H/B, B/I, H/I

Add A to the first game’s ID and B to the return match. Roll first-game sides normally. Keep terrain and rosters fixed. If practical, exchange players’ armies too, to reduce the effect of familiarity.

Afterward, repeat at least one pair with mixed chassis and heat tracks. Then change one thing at a time. Try squad size first if waiting remains a problem. To test without pilots, remove them from both armies and label that pair as a separate experiment.

What to record

Use one log per game. Leave missing measurements as “unknown.” Record setup and teaching separately from play time; include rules lookups in play time.

Question What to look for
Do infantry matter? Conventional infantry should affect scoring, denial, screening, or assaults in at least one game of each pair where used. Review pairs where they do nothing
Does position matter? Cover should help survival; exposed assaults should matter; frontal attacks on armor should remain weak
Do vehicles and ’Mechs have jobs? Record useful transport, fire support, movement, and objective play in each pair
Do squads reduce waiting? Count passes and activations after the opponent has finished. Review repeated rounds with 3 or more unanswered activations, or missions decided by holding infantry back solely to delay
Is the game a reasonable length? Aim for median play time of 120 minutes or less. Review games over 150 minutes or waits longer than 5 minutes
Is the quick reference enough? Aim for no more than 6 core-rule lookups after round 2 in a typical game. Add repeatedly missing details to the reference
Does one side have too much advantage? Compare return matches. Review a role or edge that wins all three matchup pairs without a clear army or tactical reason
Are the rules clear? Record any transport, heat, equipment, or pilot dispute needed to finish play
Are all weapons worth using? Record targets, movement, and weapon choices. Check whether Energy feels compulsory or Rockets are too safe behind buildings

Measure each player’s longest stretch without a game decision, excluding agreed breaks.

These are prompts for review, not proof of balance. Identify whether a problem comes from wording, the scenario, a pilot, or a unit before changing points.

Between games

For a rules gap, write down what happened and both players’ temporary ruling. Use that ruling in both games and mark the pair as a rules-clarification test.

Between pairs, update the version and any affected references or cards. Record formula changes and their reasons in adjustments.csv, then rebuild all costs together. Keep original printed points only as a reference.
